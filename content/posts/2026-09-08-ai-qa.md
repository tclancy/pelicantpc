## QA in the Age of AI

## Things from today

- get your shit in a kit: no excuse not to have your tooling "perfect" in the era of AI
  - Not letting package drift happen
- [Pydantic AI](https://pydantic.dev/docs/ai/overview/) - audit trails, type shapes, tool limiting, max turns
- Swiss cheese protection: max calls, expected tools, expected output shape, etc all give different chances to catch A Bad Thing

>The magician's trick requires an audience, and the last check in the pipeline is the one component you've deliberately built to not be one.

- no who watches the watchmen stuff: one agent to do the work in a sandbox, one agent to verify it all looks as expected and then it goes
through a battery of mechanical checks. Default answer is "No" until proven otherwise. No debates, no place for the agent to poison the process
- No PCRE-style extended regex. Every wildcard is a smuggling lane. Lint these things. Any use of a wildcard should be decorated
with a standard, machine-parseable comment that says who did it, why and ties back to the justification & approval
- Build chokepoints
- Use state machines, formally verify they work and there are no leaks
- Explicit typing in the narrowest possible band. Not having the exact type is another smuggling lane. `dict`, `object` whatever should
be banned. Ideally, types are enforced at compile and runtime. Consider making subtypes like `BankAccountSummary = Annotated[str, "max_length=250"]`. Just asserting the return is a string is very little security. It's not that you can't do something bad in 250 characters or less, it's that anything bad in over 250 characters sets off an alarm
- Have `unsafe` and `safe` for determining what is agent response and what is a static string from the database or whatever
